PDC F-1 Income Requirements (Verified from PDC website):

The F-1 form requires disclosure of income from:

  • “A partnership, joint venture, sole proprietorship, associations, unions, or other business or commercial entity” — if $2,400 or more
  • Employment income from any source
  • Retirement income
  • Investment income

Key threshold: $2,400. If Concord Counsel generated less than $2,400 in personal income to Lindholm during the reporting period, he may not have been required to report it. He could also argue that Concord Counsel income is “business revenue” not “personal income” — though as a sole proprietor/LLC owner, owner draws count as personal income.

We need to know: did Concord Counsel generate enough revenue to trigger the filing requirement?

Subagent verification confirms:

The PDC F-1 instructions clearly require: “Report income of $2,400 received by yourself… from a partnership, joint venture, sole proprietorship… or other business or commercial entity”

Verdict downgraded from 🔴 to 🟡 because of the $2,400 threshold gap. We can say “Lindholm’s F-1 lists no income from Concord Counsel despite his campaign website identifying him as CEO” — but we cannot say “he violated RCW 42.17” without knowing his actual business revenue.

FINDING 8: Systemic Ethics Framework Failure

ClaimVerdictSource StrengthDefense ArgumentGap
”Lakewood’s ethics framework has never required recusal”🔵 MISLEADINGSee belowRCW 42.23 requires recusal for contracts with financial interest. Lakewood has a Code of Ethics (referenced in council rules). The issue isn’t that recusal isn’t REQUIRED — it’s that it’s never ENFORCED.The claim as stated is legally incorrect. RCW 42.23 DOES require recusal for certain conflicts. Lakewood’s charter references a Code of Ethics. The problem is non-enforcement, not absence of rules.

This is our most important correction.

We’ve been saying “the ethics framework has never required recusal.” That’s false. RCW 42.23.030 says:

“No municipal officer shall be beneficially interested, directly or indirectly, in any contract which may be made by, through or under the supervision of such officer.”

And MRSC clarifies:

“When a member has a personal conflict of interest… the law requires that the member abstain from voting.”

The CORRECT framing is: “Lakewood has the legal framework for recusal, but in 16 years of meetings, no council member has ever used it.” This is actually a stronger story — it proves the system isn’t broken by design; it’s broken by culture.

Verdict: 🔵 MISLEADING as currently stated. Fix: “Lakewood’s ethics laws require recusal, but for 16 years, no council member has complied.”
