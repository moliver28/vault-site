0. Why this person or group is on the map

  • Southport Financial Services is on the map because it makes decisions and has strong ties. It is a national affordable housing developer with a Tacoma office. It works with Pierce County government on big subsidized housing projects. One example is Cedar Flats (276 units, with $7 million from the county).
  • The company focuses on Low Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) properties. LIHTC is a federal program that gives tax credits to developers who build affordable housing. That focus gives it special know-how in federal, state, and local affordable housing finance. It sits on the board of the Affordable Housing Consortium of Pierce County. That gives it a direct line into the local affordable housing policy network.
  • The graph records ties to Kelly Chambers (donated), Pierce County Council (land use), and the Chamber of Commerce (member).
  • Who is left out: Macdonald Ladd Development, LLC is named by The Urbanist as the Cedar Flats lead developer. Pierce County’s own social media names Southport as a partner. This needs to be checked. Macdonald Ladd is not on the map unless it turns out to be a partner or subsidiary. The Maureen Howard Affordable Housing Fund (a county program) is not an entity. Southport’s Tampa HQ is outside Pierce County, so it is not in this map.

3. Where their power comes from

  • Market power: Southport has developed over 120 LIHTC properties. It holds about 12,000 units in 18 states. It has “built or acquired a substantial number of market rental housing projects.” It has “a demonstrated ability to secure LIHTC allocations, as well as volume cap tax-exempt bond allocations in multiple states.” That multi-state LIHTC know-how gives it special power in affordable housing finance.
  • Pierce County presence: Southport has a Tacoma office at 1911 65th Ave W. That makes it one of the few national affordable housing developers with a physical office in Pierce County. Its VP, Zac Baker, sits on the board of the Affordable Housing Consortium of Pierce County. That gives the company a direct line into the local affordable housing policy network.
  • Government partnerships: Pierce County named Southport as a partner in the Cedar Flats groundbreaking. Cedar Flats is a 276-unit affordable housing complex in the Puyallup / South Hill / Frederickson area. The county put $7 million from the Maureen Howard Affordable Housing Fund into the project. NOTE: The Urbanist names “Macdonald Ladd Development, LLC” as the Cedar Flats lead. The relationship between Southport and Macdonald Ladd is unverified. They may be co-developers. Or Southport may be the LIHTC partner while Macdonald Ladd leads development.
  • Revenue: Unverified from public sources (private company). The scale (12,000 units, 120+ LIHTC properties, 18 states) suggests large revenue from development fees, property management, and LIHTC syndication (selling tax credits to investors).
  • Jobs: 11-50 employees across three offices. More jobs come from construction projects and property management.
  • Land and buildings: About 12,000 units in 18 states. Specific Pierce County holdings beyond Cedar Flats are unverified. The company also runs regular apartment complexes in central Florida and the southeastern U.S.

5. How they touch government

  • Cedar Flats project (Pierce County): Pierce County put $7 million from the Maureen Howard Affordable Housing Fund into Cedar Flats. It is a 276-unit affordable housing complex in the South Hill / Frederickson area. The project serves households earning less than 60% of Area Median Income (AMI) — the local middle income set by the federal government. The max income is $72,480 for a family of four. It includes larger units for families and reserves a portion for people with disabilities. The County Council approved $12.2 million for four housing projects (including Cedar Flats) at its September 2, 2025 meeting. The total Cedar Flats cost is nearly $109 million.
  • Land-use actions: Cedar Flats and other Pierce County projects need land-use approvals from Pierce County Council. The graph records a land_use tie (weight 3).
  • Campaign donations: The graph records a donation to Kelly Chambers (weight 3). Kelly Chambers (R-Puyallup) is a WA State Representative. She ran for Pierce County Executive in 2024. The amount and date from Southport or its principals to Chambers is unverified from the state’s campaign-finance agency (PDC) records.
  • LIHTC program participation: Southport’s business model is built on government programs. Those include LIHTC allocations (federal tax credits run by state housing finance agencies), tax-exempt bond allocations, and local affordable housing funds. This creates ongoing regulatory and financial ties with multiple levels of government.
  • Chamber membership: The graph records a member tie with the Chamber of Commerce (weight 4). That gives business community access and networking.
  • Lobbying: No registered lobbying activity found at the state or local level in this pass. Southport influences through its project partnerships with local government and its board seat in the Affordable Housing Consortium.

1. Who they are

  • The bottom line. Southport Financial Services has special know-how in LIHTC affordable housing finance. That is a niche but real power domain. It helps decide which projects get built in Pierce County and elsewhere. Its Tacoma office gives it a local base. Its AHC board seat (through VP Zac Baker) gives it a direct line to local affordable housing policy. Its Cedar Flats partnership with Pierce County government ($7M public investment) ties it to public money. The company’s national scale (12,000 units, 18 states, 120+ LIHTC properties) gives it capacity that local developers may lack. But its power depends on government partnerships and tax credit allocations. It does not hold any independent regulatory authority.
  • Conflicts-of-interest flags: A defense attorney would flag the overlap. Southport’s business depends on government programs (LIHTC, public housing funds). It also gave money to Kelly Chambers. That mix could look like pay-to-play. Counter: affordable housing development is a public-private partnership. Developers’ political activity is normal civic engagement. The $7M county investment in Cedar Flats went through a competitive public process by the County Council, not a sole-source deal. Southport’s LIHTC allocations come from state housing finance agencies through competitive scoring, not political discretion.
  • Analysis of alternatives: The opposing reading: Southport is a mid-sized national developer (11-50 employees). Its Pierce County presence is just one office and one known project (Cedar Flats). Its local power is modest — it is one of several affordable housing developers competing for limited county and state funds. Its actual development pipeline in Pierce County may be small. Cedar Flats may even be led by Macdonald Ladd rather than Southport, which would shrink Southport’s direct local power. This reading would place Southport’s composite power lower than rank 45/163 suggests.
