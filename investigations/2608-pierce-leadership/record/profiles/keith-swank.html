3. Where their power comes from

  • Elected office power: Pierce County Sheriff — elected countywide, 4-year term (Jan 2025 – Dec 2028). He holds statutory law-enforcement authority over unincorporated Pierce County under state law (RCW 36.28). He runs the county jail (Pierce County Detention and Corrections Center). He commands sworn deputies and civilian staff. [Source #4, #7]
  • Law-enforcement authority: Direct coercive power — arrest, detention, use of force. He commands patrol, investigations, and corrections. His authority reaches about 425,000 residents of unincorporated Pierce County. FACT.
  • Budget authority: The Sheriff’s budget is set by the Pierce County Council (graph edge: appropriates, weight 5). The exact budget is not confirmed in this pass. Past estimates were about $150–180M for the Pierce County Sheriff’s Office, including corrections.
  • Appointment and veto powers: The Sheriff hires and fires sworn personnel. He has no direct veto over council laws. But his operational independence is protected by statute.
  • Jurisdictions he runs: Unincorporated Pierce County, plus contract cities (those that contract with PCSO for law enforcement). He does NOT run the incorporated cities that have their own police departments (Tacoma, Lakewood, Puyallup, etc.).
  • Key facts:
  • Office: Pierce County Sheriff (elected, nonpartisan) [Source #4, #7].
  • Budget: Set by the County Council — exact current figure not confirmed.
  • Appointment powers: Hiring and firing of sworn and civilian staff.
  • Jurisdiction: Unincorporated Pierce County and contract cities.

7. Controversies and open questions

  • Three WSCJTC investigations (2025–2026): The Washington State Criminal Justice Training Commission (CJTC) opened three certification cases against Swank. They could lead to his decertification as a law enforcement officer. The complaints tie to his social media activity, including posts described as “hate speech” (for example, “trans women are men”). Swank called the investigation a “joke.” [Source #8, #10, #11]
  • SB 5974 conflict: Swank testified against SB 5974. The bill would set stricter eligibility rules for sheriffs and remove elected sheriffs from office if their certification is revoked. Swank told lawmakers he did not recognize their authority to impose those controls. That puts him in direct conflict with state legislative oversight. [Source #8]
  • Complaint filed (2026): A complaint (Case 2026-0000031) was filed against Swank for discrimination tied to his social media posts. [Source #10]
  • Defense: Swank and his backers would argue: (1) The WSCJTC investigations are political payback for his social media posts, which are protected First Amendment speech. (2) His CHOP/CHAZ clearance and DOJ training credentials show competence and public service. (3) SB 5974 is unconstitutional legislative overreach that weakens the elected-sheriff model. (4) The nonpartisan ballot means he is not a Republican partisan, despite GOP support. The CSPOA framing casts him as a “constitutional sheriff” with independent authority.

1. Who they are

  • Verdict: Keith Swank runs the Pierce County Sheriff’s Office — the main law-enforcement agency for unincorporated Pierce County. He holds that power because he won the November 2024 election. His authority comes from statute (RCW 36.28) and from the countywide vote. It does not depend on the County Executive or the Council (though the Council sets his budget). He won by tying together his law-enforcement credentials (33-year SPD career, CHOP clearance, DOJ training), Republican-aligned political support (Pierce GOP donation), and a populist anti-oversight posture (opposing SB 5974, challenging WSCJTC authority). Right now, three pending WSCJTC certification cases could lead to his decertification. SB 5974 would let a sheriff be removed from office after decertification.
  • Possible conflicts of interest (defense expected): The defense would say: (1) Swank’s social media posts are protected First Amendment speech. The WSCJTC investigations are political. (2) His testimony against SB 5974 is legitimate advocacy by an elected official. (3) His CHOP/CHAZ clearance shows competence, not recklessness. (4) Pierce GOP support does not make him partisan, because the office is nonpartisan. (5) The conflict with the PA Office is normal institutional tension between investigation and prosecution, not a personal fight.
  • Other ways to read this: The opposite reading is that Swank’s election was a deliberate move to install a sheriff who will resist state oversight. He uses the “constitutional sheriff” ideology to claim authority above state law. The CHOP clearance and military credentials are not just qualifications — they are political brand-building for a law-and-order populist agenda. The three WSCJTC investigations and his public defiance of legislative authority suggest a pattern of challenging institutional constraints. The Pierce GOP donation edge and the nonpartisan filing could be read as strategic partisan cover.
