0. Why this person or group is on the map

  • Why we added Kelly Chambers: She served three terms as a Washington State Representative for the 25th Legislative District, Position 1, from January 14, 2019 to January 13, 2025. She was the Republican nominee for Pierce County Executive in 2024. She lost to Ryan Mello, 48.58% to 51.28%. She is now the federally appointed SBA Pacific Northwest Regional Administrator. SBA stands for the U.S. Small Business Administration. Region 10 covers Washington, Oregon, Idaho, and Alaska. She holds office at the state level (past) and now at the federal regional level.
  • Other people we did not add: Her successor Michael Keaton (R, 25th LD) is a separate entity and not yet profiled. Her husband Jeff Chambers is a private citizen with no public role. Jamie Smith, the Democrat she faced in all three of her races, is also excluded.

3. Where their power comes from

  • Past elected office: Washington State House of Representatives, 25th Legislative District, Position 1. She served three terms (2019 to 2025). She did not run for re-election in 2024. Instead, she ran for Pierce County Executive.
  • Legislative leadership roles: Assistant Republican Whip (2018 to 2021). Republican Caucus Vice Chair (2023 to 2025). Ranking Member of the Appropriations Committee. Ranking Member of the Regulated Substances and Gaming Committee. She also served on the Healthcare and Transportation committees. A “Ranking Member” is the lead minority-party member on a committee.
  • Current federal position: SBA Pacific Northwest Regional Administrator (Region 10). She oversees SBA programs, offices, and operations across Washington, Oregon, Idaho, and Alaska. She was appointed in March 2026. This is a federal executive-branch appointment. It gives her administrative power over SBA lending, disaster relief, and small business programs in a four-state region.
  • Party: Republican Party.
  • Electoral record: She won state House races in 2018 (50.6%, 27,561 votes), 2020 (53.4%, 41,055 votes), and 2022 (55.7%, 31,366 votes). All three were against Democrat Jamie Smith. She lost the 2024 Pierce County Executive race. She got 48.58% (198,886 votes). Ryan Mello won with 51.28% (209,918 votes).

1. Who they are

  • Verdict: Kelly Chambers’ power has shifted. She moved from a state legislative role (state representative with caucus leadership) to a federal executive role (SBA Regional Administrator for 4 states). As a state legislator for three terms, she held office but had limited individual power (one of 98 representatives). Her Republican caucus leadership roles (Vice Chair, Assistant Whip, Ranking Member of Appropriations) gave her institutional influence within the minority party. Her loss in the 2024 Pierce County Executive race (by 2.7%) showed she could compete countywide but could not win the top executive office. Her SBA appointment is a jump to federal administrative power. She now controls SBA program delivery across a 4-state region. That may be more powerful in practice than her old legislative role. Her donor base is heavy on business (PACs, developers, auto dealers, construction). That matches her pro-business record and her small-business-owner identity.

  • Conflicts of interest: As SBA Regional Administrator, she oversees federal small business lending and programs. Her own businesses (Visiting Angels franchise and Lomcevak Cellars winery) are small businesses. They could in theory use SBA programs. That is a possible conflict. Federal ethics rules usually require her to step aside from matters involving her own businesses. Her wide network of business donors from her legislative career (10 or more business donors in the graph) could also raise appearance-of-conflict issues in her SBA role. This is especially true if those donors later seek SBA help. No actual conflict has been identified.

  • The other view: Chambers’ SBA appointment may just be a standard post-election patronage job for a defeated candidate. It may not be a real power gain. The Regional Administrator role is administrative, not policy-making. She carries out federal SBA policy. She does not set it. Her legislative record (three terms, no major signature legislation found) shows limited policy impact. Her county executive loss shows she could not win a countywide race even as the Republican nominee in a competitive county. Her business donors may reflect ideology rather than transactional ties.

  • Confidence: Medium — primary sources (SBA.gov, the PDC, and WA election results) confirm her positions, election results, and campaign finance. Her federal appointment is confirmed by SBA.gov and several news outlets. Personal details are well documented through Wikipedia and the News Tribune. How much real influence she has inside the SBA, and her future political path, are still uncertain.

  • Open questions & leads:.

  • Her federal ethics disclosure (OGE Form 450) for the SBA role would show whether she stepped away from Visiting Angels or Lomcevak Cellars. OGE is the federal Office of Government Ethics.

  • Her specific legislative wins (sponsored bills that became law) should be checked using BillTrack50 or Legiscan records.

  • How her SBA appointment connects to Pierce County Republican Party politics — and whether it sets her up for a future run (for example, a 2028 county executive rematch) — is worth watching.
