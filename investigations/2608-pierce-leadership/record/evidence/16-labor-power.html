2. ILWU LOCAL 23 — PORT OF TACOMA DOCKWORKERS

Overview

  • Union: International Longshore and Warehouse Union (ILWU)
  • Local: 23
  • Location: Fife / Port of Tacoma, WA
  • Jurisdiction: Longshore workers at Port of Tacoma terminals
  • Key role: Critical node in West Coast supply chain; 6th busiest container port in North America

Strike / Labor Action History

  1. 1908 strike: Employers refused to recognize Longshoremen’s Protective Club; Local 23 joined Seattle locals; led to creation of Longshoremen’s Union of the Pacific
  2. 1916 strike: Tacoma longshoremen struck and lost; lost control of dispatching
  3. 1971 strike (134 days): ILWU nationwide strike over containerization; 96.4% strike vote; longest coastwise longshore strike in U.S. history
  4. 2002 employer lockout: PMA locked out West Coast workers; ILWU Local 23 President Roger Boesflug negotiated “richest contract ever” — 58% pension increase, $3/hr wage increase; Local 23 voted 521-20 to approve
  5. 2024 WWL Wallenius strike (multiple): Feb 7, 2024 — mass strike at Vehicle Processing Center coinciding with ship offload; Aug 2, 2024 — 1-day ULP strike over illegal firing; Dec 2024 — 4-day ULP strike over firing of bargaining committee member
  6. 2025 Wallenius contract victory: Historic first contract covering 150 workers at WWL Tacoma VPC; ILWU “heat & hammer” model; longshore, truck drivers, and railroad workers honored picket lines

Political Power

  • Endorsed Nick Brown for WA Attorney General (2024)
  • ILWU international involvement in supply chain organizing (SCLT program)
  • Wallenius contract won with international solidarity from MUA (Australia), Zenkowan (Japan), ILA (East Coast)

12. CROSS-CUTTING OBSERVATIONS & NETWORK MAP

Key Power Brokers

  1. Alice Phillips (IBEW 483 / PCCLC President) — Central node connecting city employees, IBEW, and countywide labor movement
  2. Jared Faker (ILWU Local 23 President) — Controls Port of Tacoma labor; $260K+ salary; militancy model for supply chain organizing
  3. John L. Adams (LiUNA 252 / PCCLC Trustee) — Spans building trades, PCCLC leadership, and political endorsements
  4. Jared Ross (LiUNA 252 / SS BCTC Exec Secretary) — Bridge between building trades and PCCLC; also on Tacoma Streets Initiative and LEAP
  5. Michael Hines (UFCW 367 President) — 8,000 members in grocery/retail across South Sound
  6. Henry Betts (Tacoma Police Union Local 6) — Represents commissioned officers; $2,400 contribution to Chambers signals police-labor split from PCCLC
  7. Greg Bowers (SS BCTC President) — Coordinates 14 building trades unions across Pierce and South Sound

Political Alignment

  • PCCLC → Democratic candidates (Mello, Ibsen, Krishnadasan, et al.)
  • WEA-PAC → Democratic/pro-education candidates (Yambe, Schnepf, Barrans)
  • Tacoma Police Union → Split from labor consensus (Chambers donation)
  • Building Trades → Generally more moderate but affiliated with PCCLC
  • ILWU Local 23 → Militant left/labor solidarity (international ally network, “heat & hammer” model)

Financial Footprint

  • PCCLC: 44,000 members across 114 unions — dues multiplier significant
  • ILWU Local 23: President salary ~$269K; organization revenue not public (no 990)
  • Tacoma Police Local 6: ~$647K revenue; $424K program services; $206K remitted to IUPA
  • IUOE 612: $175K highest officer comp; 16 officers; 6 six-figure salaries
  • WEA-PAC: $810K statewide expenditures
