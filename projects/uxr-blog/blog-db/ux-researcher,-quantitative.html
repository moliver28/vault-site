UX Researcher, Quantitative

Greater Seattle Area | oliver.researching@gmail.com | linkedin.com/in/michaeleoliver

Summary

Seven-plus years running research end to end for enterprise SaaS, developer tools, and security: survey science, validated trust measurement, and human evaluation of AI systems. I design studies that decide things: sample plans set by precision needs, decision rules committed before fielding, and findings presented as recommendations with effect sizes and confidence, not adjectives. I read the SQL, R, and Python myself, I build AI tooling into my own workflow (LLM baselines, provenance checks), and I validate every machine-assisted output against human judgment. Recent through-line: how enterprises come to trust, or refuse, AI-driven guidance, published and practiced.

Experience

AuditBoard (SaaS, GRC)

Staff UX Researcher (first embedded UX researcher; grew the practice solo into a four-person team)

  • Built the research practice 0 to 1: generative discovery, rapid evaluative sprints, competitive benchmarking, behavioral analytics.
  • Ran 30+ interviews with customers, non-customers, and director-level stakeholders, synthesized into nine jobs to be done and three vision stories. Those became the product organization’s top three priorities and anchored a board-level plan to 3x revenue in five years.
  • Studied trust in AI-flagged risk decisions with the Critical Decision Method: why reviewers adopt, override, or ignore AI guidance. Findings became design principles for calibrated reliance, the question of whether user trust matches how reliable the system actually is.
  • Built a continuous benchmarking program that cut study cycles from eight weeks to four; directors then mandated the 80/100 usability score for every new product launch.
  • Shipped the first research repository and intake workflow, then set the standards that let non-researchers run sound templated studies themselves.
  • Designed scenario-based hiring protocols that screened for reasoning over polish; interim manager of the four-person team for six months.

GitLab (developer tools, enterprise)

Senior UX Researcher, Security

  • Designed and ran an Outcome-Driven Innovation survey that surfaced 25+ application security opportunities, prioritized across eight market segments; the prioritization changed the strategic roadmaps for three feature groups.
  • Built six security personas from 20+ interviews, each tied to benchmarkable tasks and design principles; template published for repeat use.
  • Ran the first research on GitLab Docs: two annual surveys with 1,200+ responses, followed by qualitative probes on exactly the anomalies the numbers surfaced.

Higher Logic (community SaaS)

Senior UX Researcher / UX Researcher

  • Built and validated the Engagement Benchmarking Score (EBS): an instrument grounded in expert interviews and statistical modeling, integrated into the product dashboard. Segmentation research on firmographics and add-ons fed the 2021 Engagement Benchmarking Report, which generated over $1 million ARR in its first year.
  • Rebuilt search UX from SQL usage-data analysis and rapid iterative testing: a 15-point SUS increase inside a fixed budget.
  • Ran NLP (topic modeling, sentiment) over 1,200+ open-response entries, converting free text into trackable themes.

Methods

  • Survey design with sample plans set by precision needs; instrument design and bias control; segmentation and experimental design basics, A/B testing, usability benchmarking.
  • Conjoint trade-off study designs for features, benefits, and messaging questions.
  • Interviews, Critical Decision Method, and jobs to be done for decision research; hierarchical task analysis for behavior.
  • Data science: SQL, R, Python; NLP on open text; model behavior evaluated against human baselines.
  • Research operations: repository, intake and prioritization, reusable templates, coaching non-researchers and junior researchers.

Human evaluation of AI systems

  • Benchmarked classifiers against LLM baselines for feedback labeling, with multi-annotator agreement analysis.
  • Published, October 2026: “Why UX Researchers Undertrust AI Synthesis” on the adoption-trust gap, calibrated reliance, and the provenance demands that gate AI adoption.

Education

M.A. Human Factors, George Mason University (2017, 2019)
B.A. Psychology, CSU Long Beach
