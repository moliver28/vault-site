Unanswered: no published protocol for judging AI-assisted analysis quality. - "UX evals" is becoming a name (Outset, [uxuiprinciples.com](http://uxuiprinciples.com), Microsoft's UXR org), but only vendor-side. No independent, open, evidence-based practitioner methodology exists yet. - Synthetic users are mainstream and contested: 47% of researchers skeptical, 62.7% of teams with zero guidance (User Interviews, State of Synthetic Users 2026). No published validation protocol. - Trust in automation stays the field's abstract word; I hold measured enterprise thresholds (Ledger 1; no published equivalent). - Judgment operationalization appears in job descriptions, undefined in public writing; "strategic research" likewise. - AI fatigue; NN/g 2026: "design deeper to differentiate"; generative UI matches expert-designed work 44% of the time. Differentiation runs through demonstrated business impact: this blog's argument. - Refresh rule: the weekly scan re-verifies each item; never recite a number forward without one scan verification. - Registry upgrade (October 2026, in the cron briefs): venues add Judgment and Decision Making, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, Nature Human Behaviour, Journal of Consumer Research, Behavioral & Brain Sciences (CHI, CSCW, JPSP, OBHDP, Psychological Science continue); practitioners add MeasuringU and UXmatters; canon clusters from My Niche tag academic rows at aggregation. ## How I Want to Shift the Conversation The field has drifted: research became a validation service, confirming what product teams already decided to build rather than discovering what should exist. The blogosphere mirrors it, stuck in AI anxiety, identity hand-wringing, and tools fetishism. One system claim ties the four shifts together: UX research is a subsystem of the product system, and the field drifts when its loops reward validation over discovery. The blog works the loop: measure quality at the seams, translate the science, publish frameworks openly, keep human decision making at the center. 1. **Research as a revenue driver.** UXR's generative function (discovering unmet needs, creating market categories) over validation service. Make research critical to software revenue, not a cost center confirming decisions already made. 2. **Science made usable.** The gap between academic cognitive/behavioral science and daily practice holds the most valuable content. I translate across it. 3. **Power to ICs and small teams.** Best tools give capability to individual contributors and small businesses, not enterprise hierarchies. Open frameworks, independent-practitioner-first design. 4. **Human decision making, fueled by HCI.** As AI reshapes products, stay anchored in how humans think, decide, and trust. Human Factors and cognitive psychology lead. ## My Niche One sentence: **I apply an HCI + systems lens to UX research and to the software and product industry.** Shorter: **HCI-grade rigor, systems-grade reasoning, shipped as open artifacts.** *The lens* means: HCI (cognitive psychology, decision science, human factors: how humans perceive, decide, trust, recover) plus systems thinking (feedback loops, incentives, coupling, debt: why good methods fail inside organizations). Research debt, validation-service drift, and quality gates are system interventions, not study topics; naming the broken loop is the blog's spine. Application surface: UXR's own operating system, plus the wider software industry now rebuilding workflows as human-agent systems: HCI's jurisdiction, publicly unclaimed. Framework translation: academic constructs into runnable tools (Ledger 4, 5, 8) Readers: **Primary**: research leads and senior UXR ICs at B2B/enterprise software companies who suspect methods problems are systems problems. **Secondary**: product/design leaders building AI features who need the human-decision layer engineering evals lack (Lenny sells PMs the machine-side guide; its human side is a stub). Not the reader: UX beginners, tool shoppers, consumer-trend collectors, "AI is killing UX" commentators. **Academic canon (from the NAS textbook library; the pair this into every brief):** - **Survey science**: Groves's Total Survey Error, Dillman's Tailored Design Method, the Oxford polling handbook: the formal floor under all survey/synthetic data claims. - **Agent-based social science**: Epstein's Generative Social Science and Agent Zero, Growing Artificial Societies, the ODD reporting protocol: a 20-year validation tradition for simulated agents, unknown to the UXR blogosphere: the sharpest academic counter to synthetic-user hype and panic. - **Psychometrics of ability**: WAIS-IV, Woodcock-Johnson IV, Stanford-Binet manuals: instruments built on reliability, norms, and validity: where the measurement habit comes from. - **Experimental craft + Bayesian inference**: Kohavi's Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments; Gelman's BDA, Lee/Wagenmakers Bayesian cognitive modeling: launch gates and modeled judgment. - **Computational sociology/economics + consumer psychology + behavioral economics**: modeled social systems and the demand-side lens (the library's largest shelf). - **Knowledge management and organizational innovation** (Dalkir, Oxford organizational handbooks): the systems lens in org life. - **Consciousness and philosophy of mind** (Tononi's IIT, Koch, Chalmers): the far boundary of what can be simulated: depth no AI-evals writer brings. Winnable: the lens combination is unclaimed in the UXR blogosphere (October 2026 check: research debt, tool ecosystems, AI workflow redesign stay anecdotal, single-loop, vendor-driven); 51% of researchers learn through blogs (UI 2025); enterprise/B2B is the blind spot and my daily seat. Every post maps to one shift and one territory, or it goes back. ## My Voice zen Asimov clarity through Economist/FT rigor. Calm, unhurried, committed, evidence-first, dry wit sparingly. Explain hard things in plain words, hide the effort. First person for hard-won experience, second person for guidance. Commit; no false balance. Ideas first, ego absent. Non-negotiables (edit stage enforces every one): - Zero em dashes, ever (U+2014). Commas, periods, colons, or parentheses carry the break. - Never "It's not X. It's Y." or any cousin of that construction, written-out or contracted. - Banned words: leverage, unlock, supercharge, game-changer, journey, robust (say strong), synergy, delve, tapestry, delight, best-in-class. Banned openers: "In today's rapidly evolving landscape", "Let's dive in", "it's worth noting". - Active voice. Vary rhythm: short for impact, longer for explanation, never three long in a row. No paragraph over six lines. - Headers are claims, not labels. Thesis inside the first 150 words. Close on a reframe, then a one-line punch. No summary paragraph. - Every claim carries evidence, a mechanism, or a labeled first-person receipt. No invented numbers, sources, or quotes. Unverified material gets qualified or cut. - No invented terms (Michael's standing order, 2026-10-16; protects the voice): use established field vocabulary. Define a necessary concept in plain words inside the same sentence. A coined label posing as established terminology fails the audit. Names attach only to shipped artifacts (real names, e.g. the 80/100 gate in the Ledger); future-name proposals are labeled "proposed" and never used as if established. Signature moves (two to four per piece, never all at once): the flat opener; steelman then pivot; the named-source claim with the mechanism; the receipt (first-person system or failure, told plainly); the one-line punch close; the reframe; the definition in passing. Full prose spec: the `michael-oliver-voice` skill (`skills/voice/voice-michael-oliver/SKILL.md`). On prose mechanics the skill wins; this file wins on strategic intent. ## Receipts Ledger (the writer's only supply of personal evidence) New rows: from the Career Projects DB or this profile's verified findings, opened and read first; numbers verbatim, never strengthened. Pattern: winners narrow the audience, publish original numbers, write testable titles, promote more than they write. 1. **Narrow audience**: 73% of \$50K+ bloggers focus a very specific group. Every essay carries one ledger receipt; theory pieces included. 3. **Titles**: "why" measured 37% lower CTR, "who" +22% (HubSpot); 14-17 word titles win the most shares (Backlinko, 900M posts). Mechanism + number + named artifact; first-person receipts beat theory. 4. **Naming makes methods citeable** (NN/g: banner blindness, F-pattern; Hamel: "evals"). Names attach only to artifacts I actually ship from the Ledger and the plan (e.g. the 80/100 gate); prose concepts stay in established vocabulary; coined labels posing as terms are banned (see Voice); future-name proposals get labeled as proposals. 5. **Ship artifacts, not essays**: 8x more likely among winners to offer a free tool; 53% gate a download on email. Open Expertise doubles as the subscriber strategy; the artifact circulates wider than the essay. 6. **Series over standalone** (compounding assets: email list, series, community); each part closes by ordering the next. Free for a long runway; own the list. 7. **Promote more than you write**: 70% of winners are active promoters; frequency itself rated low. Promote where the niche gathers (ResearchOps, LinkedIn essays, enterprise-AI and evals rooms); the weekly scan supplies 48-hour reaction windows; take them with data, never with panic. 8. **Define "popular" and track it**: subscribers via artifact pages, artifacts run or adapted, named-method citations, organic pull in niche rooms.
