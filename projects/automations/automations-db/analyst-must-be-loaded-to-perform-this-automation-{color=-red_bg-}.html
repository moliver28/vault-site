Discover Underserved Persona Needs — ODI Needs Analysis (Analyst in command)

Mar 28, 202618 min read

Analyst must be loaded to perform this automation {color=“red_bg”}

**Reusable ODI needs discovery for any scored population or persona.** Takes proto-personas from upstream automations, maps their core functional jobs, captures desired outcome statements, and identifies underserved needs using Ulwick's Opportunity Algorithm. Bridges secondary market evaluation with primary UX research protocols. All findings are appended non-destructively to the source market scan page. --- ## Objective Transform proto-persona sketches and market evaluation scores into validated, prioritized needs maps using Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI) methodology (Ulwick, 2005; 2016). The automation produces three outputs: (1) a secondary-research-derived opportunity landscape identifying underserved and overserved outcomes, (2) a structured primary research protocol for field validation, and (3) enriched persona profiles with jobs-to-be-done decomposition and desired outcome statements. --- ## Trigger User says something like: "map persona needs", "discover underserved needs", "run ODI analysis", "what do these personas actually need", "identify opportunity gaps", "run needs analysis on this population", "find underserved outcomes" **Upstream triggers:** Triggered from Market Evaluation Scoring Kit after scoring completes, from Identify Market Populations to Innovate For (🧭 Market Population Identifier in command) after shortlisting, or from Persist Persona Shortlist to Project (Analyst in command) when Active personas need deeper needs analysis. Can also be triggered standalone with any persona or population input. --- ### INPUT **Required:** At minimum, a persona or population with: - **Who**: Role, context, environment - **What**: Goals, tasks, workflows - **Why**: Motivations, frustrations, pain points - **Where/When**: Context of use, constraints **Optimal (from upstream chain):** - Scored population data from Market Evaluation Scoring Kit (evidence classifications, dimension scores, pre-mortem findings) - Proto-persona from Identify Market Populations to Innovate For (🧭 Market Population Identifier in command) (framework attribution, attractiveness scores, Kano landscape) - Market Scan page URL (for persistence) **Optional enrichments:** - Existing primary data (interview transcripts, survey results, observational notes) - Competitive product list for satisfaction benchmarking - Domain-specific constraints or focus areas - Project page URL (if personas are persisted via Persist Persona Shortlist to Project (Analyst in command)) --- ### STEPS **Phase 0: Context Loading & Validation** 1. **Load Analyst mode** from Mode Registry DB. 2. **Load upstream data:** If triggered from Market Eval or Identify Market Populations, load the Market Scan page and extract all relevant persona, scoring, and evidence data. 3. **Validate persona completeness:** All four dimensions (Who/What/Why/Where) must be present. If any dimension is missing or thin, STOP and ask clarifying questions. 4. **Determine data mode:** - **Secondary Only (default):** No primary data available. All outcome statements and importance/satisfaction scores will be inferred from secondary sources (web search, reviews, forums, published research, competitive analysis). Flag all outputs as "secondary-inferred." - **Primary + Secondary:** User provides interview transcripts, survey data, or observational notes. Extract outcome statements from primary data first, supplement with secondary sources. 5. **Load** Market Evaluation Scoring Kit **evidence taxonomy** for classifying all evidence gathered in this automation. **Phase 1: Define the Core Functional Job** The job-to-be-done is the anchor for all downstream analysis. Define it using Ulwick's syntax (Ulwick & Bettencourt, 2008). 1. **Extract the core functional job** from the persona's What/Why dimensions. A core functional job describes the underlying goal the persona is trying to accomplish, independent of any solution. - **Format:** \[Verb\] + \[object of the verb\] + \[contextual clarifier\] - **Example:** "Manage personal finances across multiple accounts and goals" - **Solution-agnostic test:** Would this job statement be true regardless of which product/solution the persona uses? If yes, it passes. If no, abstract further. 2. **Identify related jobs:** - **Emotional jobs:** How the persona wants to feel (or avoid feeling) while executing the core job. Format: \[Feel/Avoid feeling\] + \[emotion\] + \[context\] - **Social jobs:** How the persona wants to be perceived by others. Format: \[Be perceived as\] + \[perception\] + \[context\] - **Consumption chain jobs:** Jobs related to acquiring, learning, setting up, maintaining, and disposing of the solution itself. 3. **Present the job definition to the user for confirmation.** A misframed job cascades errors through every downstream phase. **Phase 2: Create the Job Map** The job map is the backbone of ODI. It decomposes the core functional job into process steps that are universal across all job executors, regardless of solution (Ulwick & Bettencourt, HBR 2008). 1. **Map the core job to the Universal Job Map.** Every job follows this sequence:
**Step****Job Step****What the Persona Does****Key Question**
1**Define**Determine goals, plan approach, assess resources neededWhat must the persona define or plan before starting?
2**Locate**Gather inputs, find information, collect materials neededWhat inputs must the persona find or gather?
3**Prepare**Set up environment, organize inputs, ready for executionHow must the persona organize or set up before executing?
4**Confirm**Validate readiness, verify inputs are correct and completeWhat must the persona verify before proceeding?
5**Execute**Carry out the core taskWhat is the central action the persona performs?
6**Monitor**Track progress, assess whether execution is going wellHow does the persona know if things are on track?
7**Modify**Make adjustments, handle exceptions, correct courseWhat changes when something goes wrong or conditions shift?
8**Conclude**Finalize, store results, clean up, review outcomesHow does the persona finish and evaluate success?
1. **Populate each step** with the specific activities the persona performs for their core functional job. Use web search for behavioral evidence: forum posts, reviews, workaround descriptions, workflow screenshots, how-to guides, community discussions. 2. **Validate solution-independence:** Every step must describe what the persona does, not how any specific product helps them do it. If a step mentions a product name, abstract it. **Phase 3: Capture Desired Outcome Statements** Desired outcomes are the metrics personas use to measure success at each job step. They are the innovation currency of ODI (Ulwick, 2016). 1. **For each job map step, capture 5-10 desired outcome statements** using the ODI format: **\[Direction of improvement\] + \[performance metric\] + \[object of control\] + \[contextual clarifier\]** - **Direction:** Minimize or Maximize (never "optimize" or "improve") - **Metric:** Time, likelihood, number, or ability - **Object of control:** The specific thing being measured - **Clarifier:** The context in which the outcome matters **Examples:** - "Minimize the time it takes to identify which financial accounts need attention" - "Minimize the likelihood of overlooking a recurring expense that could be reduced" - "Maximize the ability to compare investment options using consistent criteria" 2. **Source hierarchy for outcome extraction (ordered by reliability):** - **Primary data (if available):** Interview transcripts, contextual inquiry notes, survey open-ends. Extract outcomes directly from user language. Convert to ODI format. - **Behavioral evidence (Tier 2):** Forum posts, Reddit threads, product reviews, community discussions where personas describe what they struggle with. - **Competitive analysis (Tier 2):** Feature reviews, churn reasons, support tickets, app store reviews. Convert complaints into desired outcomes. - **Declared evidence (Tier 3):** Surveys, stated preferences, feature request boards. Discount per Market Evaluation Scoring Kit taxonomy. - **Published research (Tier 3-4):** Academic papers, industry reports, UX case studies. - **Inference (Tier 4, last resort):** Logical deduction from job map steps. Flag explicitly as "inferred, requires validation." 3. **Also capture:** - **Emotional outcome statements** for emotional jobs: "Minimize the likelihood of feeling anxious when \[context\]" - **Social outcome statements** for social jobs: "Maximize the likelihood that \[audience\] views my \[action\] as \[perception\]" - **Consumption chain outcomes** for related jobs: "Minimize the time it takes to learn how to use \[solution category\]" 4. **Quality gate:** Each outcome statement must be: (a) solution-agnostic, (b) stable over time, (c) measurable, (d) controllable, and (e) unambiguous. Review all statements against these five criteria before proceeding. 5. **Present the full outcome set** (organized by job map step) to the user for review before scoring. **Phase 4: Score Outcomes — The Opportunity Algorithm** This is where ODI transforms qualitative needs into quantitative priorities (Ulwick, 2005). 1. **For each desired outcome, estimate two scores (1-10 scale):** - **Importance:** How critical is this outcome to the persona? (10 = essential, can't get the job done without it; 1 = irrelevant) - **Satisfaction:** How well do current solutions satisfy this outcome? (10 = perfectly satisfied, 1 = completely unsatisfied) 2. **Scoring evidence sources (ranked by reliability):** - **Primary quantitative data (gold standard):** Survey data where respondents rated importance and satisfaction. This is how ODI is designed to work at scale. - **Primary qualitative data:** Interview emphasis, emotional intensity, frequency of mention, workaround effort invested. Convert to estimated scores with explicit reasoning. - **Secondary behavioral evidence (Tier 2):** Workaround prevalence, review complaint frequency, competitive feature adoption rates. - **Secondary declared evidence (Tier 3):** Forum complaints, stated preferences, feature requests. Discount per evidence taxonomy (Tier 3 caps from Market Eval apply). - **Inference (Tier 4):** Expert judgment from competitive analysis and domain knowledge. Flag explicitly. 3. **Apply the Opportunity Algorithm** (Ulwick, 2005; Strategyn): **Opportunity Score = Importance + max(Importance − Satisfaction, 0)** Scale: 0-20. The formula double-weights importance when satisfaction is low, surfacing outcomes that are both critical and poorly addressed. 4. **Classify each outcome:**
**Score****Classification****Strategic Implication**
**\>12****Underserved**High-value innovation target. Persona cares deeply and current solutions fail. Prioritize.
**10-12****Appropriately Served**Current solutions handle this adequately. Match parity but don't over-invest.
**\<10 (high sat, low imp)****Overserved**Current solutions over-deliver. Opportunity to strip cost (disruptive innovation territory).
**High imp, high sat****Table Stakes**Must satisfy at parity. Not a differentiator, but failure here is fatal.
1. **Build the Opportunity Landscape:** Rank all outcomes by opportunity score, descending. The top 10 underserved outcomes are the primary innovation targets. **Phase 5: Synthesize the Underserved Needs Profile** 1. **Cluster underserved outcomes by job map step.** Which steps have the highest concentration of underserved outcomes? These are "pain clusters" where current solutions fail most. 2. **Cross-reference with upstream data (when available):** - **Market Eval scores:** Do underserved outcomes align with dimensions that scored highest in Problem Severity? Flag discrepancies. - **Kano landscape (from upstream):** Underserved outcomes on Attractive Quality dimensions = highest-impact opportunities. Underserved outcomes on Must-Be dimensions = market failures. - **Pre-mortem findings:** Do any underserved outcomes relate to the most likely failure mode identified in Market Eval? If so, flag as a validation signal. 3. **Generate the Underserved Needs Summary:** - Top 10 underserved outcomes ranked by opportunity score - Pain cluster analysis (which job steps are most underserved) - Evidence basis for each outcome (evidence tier, source type) - Cross-references with Market Eval dimensions (if available) 4. **Generate the Overserved Needs Summary:** Identify outcomes where current solutions over-deliver relative to importance. These represent opportunities for disruptive simplification or cost reduction. **Phase 6: Primary Research Protocol** This phase produces a structured research plan the user can execute to validate secondary-inferred findings with primary data. Even when secondary analysis is strong, the protocol provides the path from "promising signal" to "validated opportunity." 1. **Contextual Inquiry Protocol** (Holtzblatt & Beyer, 2014; NN/g): - **Objective:** Observe the persona executing the core functional job in natural context - **Participant screener:** Derived from persona Who dimension + earlyvangelist criteria (has the problem, knows it, actively seeks solutions, has budget/authority) - **Observation guide (one section per job map step):** - What tools/workarounds does the participant use? - Where do they hesitate, backtrack, or express frustration? - What information do they seek but cannot find? - What do they do differently from what they describe? - **Sample size:** 8-12 participants for pattern saturation 2. **ODI Interview Guide:** - **Structure:** Walk through the job map step by step with participants - **Per step, ask:** - "When you \[job step\], what are you trying to achieve?" - "How do you know when \[job step\] is going well?" - "What makes \[job step\] take longer than it should?" - "What causes \[job step\] to go wrong?" - "What would 'perfect' look like for \[job step\]?" - **Outcome extraction rule:** Convert every response into a desired outcome statement using ODI format. Validate format in real-time during the interview. 3. **Importance-Satisfaction Survey Design:** - **Instrument:** For each captured desired outcome, two questions: - "How important is it to you to \[outcome statement\]?" (1 = Not at all important, 10 = Extremely important) - "How satisfied are you with your current ability to \[outcome statement\]?" (1 = Not at all satisfied, 10 = Extremely satisfied) - **Sample:** Minimum n=50 for statistical reliability; n=100+ for needs-based segmentation - **Distribution channels:** Derived from persona Where dimension (communities, forums, platforms identified in upstream scan) 4. **Triangulation Matrix:**
**Method****What It Validates****Evidence Tier (per Market Eval taxonomy)**
Contextual InquiryJob map accuracy, workaround behavior, implicit needsTier 2 (Behavioral)
ODI InterviewsOutcome statement completeness, emotional/social jobsTier 3 (Declared) → Tier 2 if observed
Importance-Satisfaction SurveyOpportunity scores with statistical validityTier 1 (Market) when n≥50
Competitive Review AnalysisSatisfaction benchmarks, feature gap validationTier 2 (Behavioral)
 --- ## Persist to Source Market Scan Page After analysis is complete, **append the ODI needs analysis to the source scan page** in . This is non-destructive: existing scan content is never overwritten, reordered, or deleted. 1. **Locate the source scan page.** If triggered from Market Evaluation Scoring Kit or Identify Market Populations to Innovate For (🧭 Market Population Identifier in command), use the scan page from their output. If standalone, ask the user which scan page to append to (or create a new one). 2. **Append** a `## 🔬 ODI Needs Analysis` section to the **end** of the scan page content (after the `## 🎯 Market Evaluation` section if it exists). Include: - Core functional job definition (functional, emotional, social, consumption chain) - Job map (8-step table with persona-specific activities) - Desired outcome statements organized by job map step (with evidence tier for each) - Opportunity Landscape table (all outcomes with importance, satisfaction, opportunity score, classification) - Underserved Needs Summary (top 10 outcomes, pain clusters, cross-references with Market Eval) - Overserved Needs Summary (disruptive simplification opportunities) - Primary Research Protocol summary (contextual inquiry plan, interview guide, survey design) - Evidence confidence assessment (what is secondary-inferred vs. primary-validated) 3. **Update scan page property:** Set `Needs Mapped` to `true`. 4. **Format for dual readability:** human-scannable (callouts, tables, color coding) and machine-parsable (structured headings, consistent naming). **NEVER overwrite or modify existing scan content.** The ODI section is always appended after existing content. If a `## 🔬 ODI Needs Analysis` section already exists (re-run), replace only that section. --- ## Persona Persistence Offer After the opportunity landscape is complete and before the downstream handoff, ask: *"Would you like to update the persisted personas with the ODI needs data (JTBD decomposition, top underserved outcomes, evidence classifications)?"* If yes, hand off to Persist Persona Shortlist to Project (Analyst in command) with the enriched persona data and the Market Scan page URL as Source. --- ### Chained Automations ⬆️ **Upstream:** - Market Evaluation Scoring Kit (handoff with scored population data, evidence classifications, pre-mortem findings) - Identify Market Populations to Innovate For (🧭 Market Population Identifier in command) (direct from Phase 6 with proto-persona and attractiveness scores) - Persist Persona Shortlist to Project (Analyst in command) (when Active personas need deeper needs analysis) - Can also be triggered standalone with any persona input ⬇️ **Downstream (all gated):** - Persist Persona Shortlist to Project (Analyst in command) (update personas with ODI needs data) - Validate Business Idea (enriched with underserved outcomes and JTBD decomposition) - Ideation Engine: Business Idea or Feature (💡 Feature Ideator in command) (underserved outcomes feed directly into Needs Decomposition and HMW phases) 🔄 **Handoff type:** `gated` (pauses for user confirmation after opportunity landscape is complete) --- **Handoff Contracts:** **⬆️ From Market Evaluation Scoring Kit** - **Trigger:** User confirms ODI Needs Analysis handoff after scoring completes - **Payload received:** Scored population data (evidence classifications, dimension scores, pre-mortem findings, validation confidence block), Market Scan page URL - **Validation gate:** Scoring complete with all 8 dimensions rated; at least one population with complete proto-persona **⬆️ From Identify Market Populations (direct)** - **Trigger:** User confirms ODI Needs Analysis handoff at Phase 6 - **Payload received:** Proto-persona (Who/What/Why/Where), framework attribution, attractiveness scores - **Validation gate:** Proto-persona has all four dimensions populated **⬆️ From Persist Persona Shortlist** - **Trigger:** User requests deeper needs analysis on Active personas - **Payload received:** Persona Shortlist section content, Project page URL - **Validation gate:** At least one Active persona with all four dimensions **→ Validate Business Idea** - **Trigger:** User confirms Validate Business handoff after ODI analysis - **Payload:** Persona + JTBD decomposition (core, emotional, social jobs) + job map + top underserved outcomes (with opportunity scores and evidence classifications) + primary research protocol status - **Validation gate:** ODI analysis complete; at least 5 underserved outcomes identified; user confirmation received **→ Ideation Engine** - **Trigger:** User confirms Ideation handoff after ODI analysis - **Payload:** Persona + JTBD decomposition + underserved outcomes (feeds directly into Needs Decomposition Phase 2, replacing inference with ODI data) + overserved outcomes (informs Kano landscape) + job map - **Validation gate:** ODI analysis complete; at least 5 desired outcomes captured; user confirmation received **→ Persist Persona Shortlist** - **Trigger:** User confirms persona enrichment after ODI analysis - **Payload:** Original persona + JTBD decomposition + top 5 underserved outcomes + evidence tier per outcome + Market Scan page URL as Source - **Validation gate:** JTBD decomposition complete; at least one persona with all four dimensions + job definition --- ### Rules - **NEVER skip the job map.** The job map is the backbone of ODI. Every desired outcome must trace to a job map step. - **NEVER capture outcome statements that reference a specific solution or product.** Outcomes must be solution-agnostic and stable over time. - **NEVER present opportunity scores without explicit evidence classification** per the Market Evaluation Scoring Kit taxonomy. Every importance and satisfaction estimate must cite its evidence source and tier. - **ALWAYS use the ODI desired outcome statement format:** Direction of improvement + Performance metric + Object of control + Contextual clarifier. - **ALWAYS distinguish secondary-inferred scores from primary-validated scores.** When all scoring is secondary-inferred, flag the confidence ceiling as "Moderate" and note this limitation. - **ALWAYS include the Primary Research Protocol** in output, even when secondary analysis is strong. The protocol is the path from "promising signal" to "validated opportunity." - **ALWAYS present the job definition** (Phase 1) for user confirmation before proceeding. A misframed job cascades errors through all downstream phases. - **ALWAYS present the full outcome set** (Phase 3) for user review before scoring. Missing outcomes cannot be discovered after scoring. - **ALWAYS cross-reference ODI findings with upstream Market Eval data** when available. Flag and analyze discrepancies between Market Eval dimension scores and ODI opportunity scores. - **NEVER overwrite or modify existing scan content.** The ODI section is always appended after existing content. - **ALWAYS use web search** to ground outcome statements and scoring in real evidence. No speculation. - **ALWAYS offer the Phase 8 handoff** so the user can proceed to downstream automations or primary research. --- ### Output 1. **Core Functional Job Definition** (functional + emotional + social + consumption chain jobs) 2. **Job Map** (8-step universal map populated with persona-specific activities) 3. **Desired Outcome Statements** (organized by job map step, with evidence tier per outcome) 4. **Opportunity Landscape** (all outcomes scored: importance, satisfaction, opportunity score, classification) 5. **Underserved Needs Summary** (top 10 outcomes, pain clusters by job step, cross-references with Market Eval) 6. **Overserved Needs Summary** (disruptive simplification opportunities) 7. **Primary Research Protocol** (contextual inquiry plan, ODI interview guide, importance-satisfaction survey design, triangulation matrix) 8. **Evidence Confidence Assessment** (secondary-inferred vs. primary-validated, confidence ceiling, key assumptions) 9. **Handoff Options** (Validate Business, Ideation Engine, Persist Personas, Primary Research, Another Population) **Confidence Ceiling by Data Mode:**
**Data Mode****Ceiling****Interpretation**
Secondary only**Moderate**Opportunity scores are directional, not statistically valid. Useful for prioritization, not final product decisions.
Primary qualitative + secondary**High-Moderate**Outcome completeness is validated, scores are informed estimates.
Primary quantitative (n≥50) + secondary**High**Opportunity scores are statistically meaningful. This is the ODI gold standard.
