10 Strategic Content Topics: Influencing the UX Conversation

10 Strategic Content Topics: Influencing the UX Conversation

Nov 13, 202612 min read

The Landscape (March 2026)

The UX Medium/blogger space is stuck in three ruts:

  1. The AI anxiety loop. “AI is replacing us” → “No it isn’t” → “Here’s how I use ChatGPT” → repeat. Surface-level, anecdotal, rarely grounded in actual cognitive or behavioral science.
  2. The identity crisis. “UX is dead,” “the return of the generalist,” “design deeper to differentiate.” NN/g, Maze, and UX Collective are all circling the same existential question without offering operational answers.
  3. The tools fetish. Endless posts about AI-assisted synthesis, Figma workflows, and prompt engineering for researchers. Useful but commoditized. No moat.
    What’s missing: Rigorous, framework-driven thinking that connects cognitive science to practitioner reality. Content that treats UX professionals as intelligent adults who deserve more than listicles. Writing that pushes the field forward rather than just narrating what’s happening to it.
    That’s your lane.

Your Positioning Advantage

**Asset****Why It's Rare in the Blogosphere**

Systematic Literature Review capabilityAlmost no one in the practitioner space synthesizes academic literature. Your SLR work on AI readiness, trust, and usability/churn is a genuine differentiator.
B2B/Enterprise AI + GRC domainThe conversation is dominated by consumer product examples. Enterprise AI trust, compliance workflows, and agentic AI adoption are underrepresented and high-value.

--- ## The 10 Topics Each topic is designed to: (a) tap a live conversation with high engagement potential, (b) leverage your specific expertise in ways competitors can't replicate, and (c) push the industry conversation toward your vision of research-driven innovation and open expertise. --- ### 1. The Terrain of Judgment: Why the Real Edge in AI Isn't Deciding, It's Learning **The conversation right now:** Everyone from NN/g to Maze to Lyssna says "human judgment is the edge" in AI-augmented research. 69% of researchers use AI in some projects. But *nobody is operationalizing what judgment means.* It's a hand-wave, and the premise is wrong: agentic systems can automate judgments. The architect decides which judgments are human and which are machine. **Your angle:** Judgment is the shared terrain of human and AI, not a wall between them. Use dual-process theory (Kahneman), recognition-primed decision making (Klein), ecological rationality (Gigerenzer), and double-loop learning (Argyris & Schön) to argue that the real gap is **learning from the system's judgments**, not making individual calls. Expand from research into product development, organizational navigation, career strategy, academic contribution, and mentoring. Address the two-way trust risk of shifting terrain and the orchestration paradox (managing AI vs. learning from it). **Why only you:** Your decision science + cognitive psychology foundation lets you move from platitude to precision. This builds directly on your Four Mental Models That Transform How You Use AI , Research in the Age of AI: Using Decision Science to Build LLM Personas That Actually Think, and . **Directional push:** Reframes the AI debate from replacement anxiety to expanded human vision. Gives practitioners a new way to see, not just a framework to follow. Advances "power to ICs" and "human decision making fueled by HCI" from Content Direction. **Status:** First draft complete: Human Judgment Is the Edge. Stop Guarding It, Start Architecting It. --- ### 2. Research for Innovation Is Dead. Here's How to Resurrect It. **The conversation right now:** NN/g's "State of UX 2026" says "design deeper to differentiate" and "UI is no longer a differentiator." Maze reports research reaching the boardroom. But the dominant model is still research-as-validation: test what product already decided to build. **Your angle:** A manifesto-style piece arguing that UXR abandoned its most powerful function (generative research that discovers unmet needs and creates new market categories) in favor of becoming a usability testing service. Trace the historical drift. Show what was lost. Provide a concrete operational model for reintroducing innovation research into product orgs. **Why only you:** This *is* your thesis. "Changing the landscape of User Experience Research by returning to its roots of Research for Market/User Innovation." Nobody else in the blogosphere is making this specific argument with your level of methodological depth. **Directional push:** Directly advances your core mission. Positions you as the voice for a research renaissance. --- ### 3. The Democratization Trap: Why "Everyone Can Do Research" Fails Without Decision Frameworks **The conversation right now:** Maze reports more non-researchers running studies while "systems, standards, and enablement lag behind, creating bottlenecks and inconsistent data quality." The debate is polarized: gatekeepers vs. democratizers. **Your angle:** Neither side is right. Democratization fails not because non-researchers are incompetent, but because we hand them methods without decision frameworks. Build on your "open expertise" principle: the goal isn't gatekeeping *or* free-for-all. It's distributing the *thinking tools* (when to use which method, how to spot your own confirmation bias, how to know when your sample is lying to you) alongside the procedural tools. **Why only you:** Your open expertise philosophy + framework-building instinct + actual experience mentoring non-researchers (your team, stakeholders at AuditBoard). This is also where your cognitive psychology training pays off: you can identify the specific cognitive biases that untrained researchers fall into. **Directional push:** Advances the "open expertise" pillar. Positions frameworks as the mechanism for democratization. Makes expertise accessible without making it trivial. --- ### 4. Trust Architecture: A Cognitive Science Framework for Designing AI Products People Actually Use **The conversation right now:** CMSWire declares "Trust is the new benchmark for AI, and UX owns the outcome." Forbes talks about human-agent ecosystems. But the conversation is all assertion, no architecture. How do you *actually design* for trust in AI products? **Your angle:** Synthesize your AI trust/readiness research at AuditBoard with the broader academic literature (your SLR on validated measures of AI readiness and trust). Build a practitioner-facing trust architecture: the cognitive components of trust in AI systems, how they map to design decisions, and how to measure whether your trust design is working. **Why only you:** You literally run this research program. and give you a foundation nobody in the blogosphere has. Enterprise B2B context makes it even rarer. **Directional push:** Elevates the trust conversation from buzzword to operational framework. Shows what research-driven design looks like. --- ### 5. The Measurement Crisis: How Do You Benchmark UX When AI Generates the Interface? **The conversation right now:** Generative UI is here. Gemini 3 Pro generates interfaces that match expert-designed work 44% of the time. Dynamic, personalized interfaces rendered on demand. NN/g says designers will "craft constraints and evaluation criteria." But *what criteria?* How do you measure quality when every user gets a different interface? **Your angle:** Your quantitative measurement expertise (SUS, benchmarking, UMUX, CSAT) meets a problem nobody has solved. Build a framework for measuring UX quality in generative/dynamic interfaces: what traditional metrics still apply, what breaks, and what new measurement approaches are needed. Draw on your The Last Usability Benchmarking Template you’ll need and The UX Measurement Landscape: Choosing the Right Metrics for the Right Questions work. **Why only you:** Most bloggers writing about generative UI are designers, not measurement people. You bring the quantitative rigor that's missing from this conversation. **Directional push:** Keeps research methodology central to the AI design conversation. Prevents measurement from being an afterthought. --- ### 6. Agentic AI Needs Agentic Research: A Methodology for Studying Human-Agent Ecosystems **The conversation right now:** 60% of designers believe AI agents will have major impact in 2026. Forbes describes "human-agent ecosystems" requiring new design patterns. But nobody is asking: how do you *research* these ecosystems? Traditional usability testing doesn't work when the AI is a co-actor. **Your angle:** Propose a research methodology adapted for agentic AI. Draw on Human Factors (your background), distributed cognition theory, and joint cognitive systems to create a framework for studying how humans and AI agents collaborate, fail, and recover. Your work with agentic AI workflows at AuditBoard (DIP Editor, AI summaries, fieldwork automation) gives you real examples. **Why only you:** Human Factors + enterprise AI product experience + cognitive systems theory. This is the intersection of your academic training and your daily work. Almost no one in the UX blogosphere has Human Factors depth. **Directional push:** Positions research methodology as essential to the agentic AI wave. Prevents the field from designing agents without studying them. --- ### 7. The Strategic Research Playbook: Turning "Be More Strategic" Into Actual Operations **The conversation right now:** Everyone says research needs to be "more strategic." Maze reports research reaching the boardroom. NN/g says develop "deep UX skills." But these are aspirations, not operations. Practitioners are left asking: *how?* **Your angle:** Build the playbook. You operated as both IC and de facto research leader, ran the discipline's daily operations, and presented connected risk findings from IC to executive level. Codify what "strategic research" actually looks like operationally: how to identify strategic questions vs. tactical ones, how to structure a research program (not just projects), how to translate findings into executive language, and how to measure research's organizational impact. **Why only you:** You've *done this*. Your performance review literally says you "advanced connected risk vision with substantive research data" across every stakeholder level. This isn't theory for you. **Directional push:** Makes strategic research concrete and accessible. Advances your medium-term goal of becoming the go-to voice for scaling research operations. --- ### 8. Epistemic Humility in AI-Assisted Synthesis: When to Trust the Machine and When to Override It **The conversation right now:** 88% of researchers say AI-assisted analysis is the top trend. 23% use AI for analysis and pattern-finding. But the conversation is binary: "AI synthesis is amazing" vs. "AI synthesis is dangerous." Nobody is building the decision framework for *when* to trust it. **Your angle:** Build on your epistemic heuristics research () to create a practitioner framework for calibrating trust in AI-generated insights. When does the pattern the AI found reflect reality vs. reflect training data? What are the specific epistemic failure modes? How do you build "verification checkpoints" into an AI-augmented synthesis workflow? **Why only you:** Epistemic heuristics + decision science + hands-on AI workflow experience. You're one of very few people who can bridge philosophy of knowledge with practical UX workflow design. **Directional push:** Elevates the AI-in-research conversation from "should we?" to "how do we think clearly about this?" Advances responsible, rigorous AI adoption. --- ### 9. The Cognitive Load of AI: Why "More Features" Is Failing Users and What Human Factors Tells Us to Do Instead **The conversation right now:** NN/g reports "AI fatigue." Users are overwhelmed. Products ship AI features nobody asked for. The UX conversation is starting to acknowledge this, but solutions are vague: "design simpler," "be intentional." **Your angle:** Apply cognitive load theory (Sweller), attention economics, and Human Factors principles to build a diagnostic framework for AI feature overload. When does an AI feature reduce cognitive load vs. add it? How do you evaluate the net cognitive cost of an AI capability? Use your GRC/audit domain to show real examples: when AI summaries help vs. when they create a false sense of understanding in compliance workflows. **Why only you:** Human Factors training + cognitive psychology + enterprise AI product context. This reframes a design problem as a cognitive science problem, which is exactly where you thrive. **Directional push:** Returns the AI product conversation to human cognition. Centers Human Factors as the discipline that should be leading this discussion. --- ### 10. Beyond Validation: A Decision Science Framework for Knowing *What* to Build **The conversation right now:** The "Double Diamond is dead" discourse is alive. Your own piece (The Double Diamond is Dead. Long Live the Double Diamond) already pushed this conversation. But the follow-up question remains: if the existing process models are broken, what replaces them for the hardest question in product development, *what should we build?* **Your angle:** A sequel/companion to your Double Diamond piece. Use decision science (naturalistic decision making, recognition-primed decision making, pre-mortem analysis) to build a generative research framework specifically designed for the "what to build" question. Not another process diamond. A *decision architecture* that helps product teams identify where they're using intuition when they should use evidence, and vice versa. **Why only you:** Decision science is your intellectual home. This directly extends your existing published work and your We should go beyond being just researchers, knowledge acquisition experts thesis. Nobody else in the UX blogosphere is building decision architectures. **Directional push:** Culmination of your "Research for Innovation" mission. Gives the field a concrete alternative to broken process models. --- ## Publishing Strategy
