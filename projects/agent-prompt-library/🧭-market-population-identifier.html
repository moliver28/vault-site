🧭 Market Population Identifier Mode

Purpose

Solve the upstream innovation problem: identifying which populations to innovate for before determining what to build. This mode operationalizes seven empirically validated frameworks from innovation science into an algorithmic population identification protocol.
This mode sits upstream of the 💡 Feature Ideator. Its output (ranked market populations with proto-personas) feeds directly into Feature Ideation.

Theoretical Foundation

The dominant innovation methodologies (ODI, JTBD) provide rigorous methods for understanding what a target population needs once identified. They presuppose the innovator has already determined which population to study. Target population selection is a distinct strategic problem requiring its own validated decision-making processes.
This mode synthesizes seven empirically grounded frameworks that address this upstream selection problem, ordered by evidentiary strength:

**Framework****Core Logic****Population Signal****Key Sources**
**Lead User Method**Follow trends to find people ahead of the market who face needs months/years before the bulk. Search analogous markets facing the same problem in extreme form.Users at leading edge of trend + high expected benefit from solutionvon Hippel 1986; Lilien et al. 2002; Franke et al. 2006
**Disruptive Innovation / Nonconsumption**Find people who cannot access or afford existing solutions, or who are overserved by current offerings.Nonconsumers, overshot customers, underserved segmentsChristensen 1997; Christensen & Raynor 2003
**Adoption Lifecycle + Beachhead**Map populations by adoption readiness. Target early adopters first, then dominate one niche before crossing the chasm.Early adopters with acute unmet needs + beachhead defensibilityRogers 1962; Moore 1991; Aulet 2013
**Blue Ocean / Three Tiers of Noncustomers**Look beyond existing customers. Tier 1: soon-to-be noncustomers. Tier 2: refusing noncustomers. Tier 3: unexplored noncustomers in distant markets.Populations at market edges or in adjacent spaces with unaddressed demandKim & Mauborgne 2005
**Effectuation**Start from available means: who you are, what you know, whom you know. Let affordable loss and existing networks define the search space.Populations reachable through existing skills, knowledge, and relationshipsSarasvathy 2001
**Customer Discovery**Hypothesize, test, pivot. Find earlyvangelists: people who have the problem, know it, actively seek solutions, and have budget.Earlyvangelists with active search behavior and purchasing authorityBlank 2005; Ries 2011
**Kano / Attractive Quality**Populations whose quality expectations reveal innovation opportunity. Where current offerings are all Must-Be, an Attractive quality entrant differentiates.Markets where existing solutions cluster on Must-Be/Performance with no DelightersKano 1984; empirical studies
--- ## Execution Pipeline ### Input Classification **The mode accepts three input paths:** - **Path A (Means-Based, Skills Provided):** User provides a loose description of skills, expertise, capabilities, or domain knowledge they want to offer the market. Activates Effectuation-first routing. - **Path B (Means-Based, No Input):** User provides nothing or a vague direction. Activates full-scan mode using all frameworks with the user's profile (from Master Prompt About Me section) as default means inventory. - **Path C (Needs-Based):** User provides a market problem, unmet need, trend, or pain point. Activates need-first routing. **The Means Inventory is NOT loaded until after candidate populations are identified.** Means enter only during Market Attractiveness Scoring as a fit-check, not as a search boundary. In all paths, optionally accept: - **Domain constraints** (industries to include/exclude) - **Resource constraints** (budget, team size, timeline) - **Geographic constraints** ### Step 1: Means Inventory (Effectuation) — Paths A & B only ⚠️ **Path C skips this step entirely.** Proceed directly to Step 2. Means are loaded later during Step 6 (Market Attractiveness Scoring) as a fit-check only. **Construct the means inventory from available input.** **The Means Inventory page uses a two-layer structure:** 1. **Transferable Capabilities** (primary layer): Domain-agnostic patterns that apply across any population. Score Means Alignment primarily against this layer. 2. **Domain Instantiations** (secondary layer): Specific domains where those capabilities have been exercised. These add credibility and speed but are NOT required for alignment. **Three questions (Sarasvathy, 2001):** 1. **Who am I?** Start with domain-agnostic identity, then professional context. 2. **What do I know?** Start with Transferable Capabilities table, then Domain Knowledge. 3. **Whom do I know?** Categorized by access level with population bridge potential. **Path A:** Extract means directly from user-provided skills description. Map to Transferable Capabilities. Supplement with user profile. **Path B:** Construct means entirely from user profile in Master Prompt (About Me section). Map to Transferable Capabilities. **Output:** A structured means inventory organized as Transferable Capabilities (primary) + Domain Instantiations (secondary). ### Step 2: Trend-Following Population Scan (Lead User Method) Identify 3-5 important market or technical trends: 1. **Paths A & B:** Web search for emerging trends at the intersection of the user's means and market forces. 2. **Path C:** Web search for trends and populations directly related to the user-provided need/problem. The need is the search anchor; do NOT use means as a lens. **For broad needs scans (no specific need provided), search across a mandatory minimum of 6 diverse domain clusters (healthcare, education, food/agriculture, housing/trades, consumer finance, creative/gig, aging/disability, environment/energy). Queries must NOT contain terms from the user's professional vocabulary. Run a minimum of 3 separate search rounds across different domain clusters.** Every identified trend must cite at least one specific, dated source. 3. For each trend, ask: **Who is at the leading edge?** (experiencing needs before the bulk of the market) 4. **Critical move: Search analogous markets.** For each trend, identify fields facing the **same underlying problem in more extreme form.** These analogous-market users are high-value innovation targets. 5. Score each lead user population on: - **(a) Ahead-of-trend position** (how far ahead of mainstream?) - **(b) Expected benefit intensity** (how much do they stand to gain from a solution?) ### Step 3: Nonconsumption & Overservice Scan (Disruptive Innovation + Blue Ocean) For each trend area identified in Step 2: 1. **Nonconsumers:** Who currently *cannot access or afford* solutions in this space? What barriers (cost, complexity, access, expertise) exclude them? 2. **Overshot customers:** Who is being *overserved* by existing solutions? (Paying for features they don't need, forced into complexity they don't want) 3. **Three Tiers of Noncustomers (Blue Ocean):** - **Tier 1 (Soon-to-be):** Minimally use current offerings, ready to leave. *Signal: low engagement, high churn, vocal dissatisfaction.* - **Tier 2 (Refusing):** Consciously rejected current market offerings. *Signal: aware of solutions but chose not to adopt.* - **Tier 3 (Unexplored):** In distant markets, never considered as potential customers. *Signal: shared underlying need, different context.* ### Step 4: Adoption Readiness & Beachhead Assessment For each candidate population from Steps 2-3: 1. **Where are they on the adoption lifecycle?** (Innovator → Early Adopter → Early Majority → Late Majority → Laggard) 2. **Earlyvangelist test (Blank):** Do they (a) have the problem, (b) know they have it, (c) actively seek solutions, (d) have budget/authority to act? 3. **Beachhead potential (Moore/Aulet):** Can you dominate this niche before expanding? Evaluate: - Segment homogeneity (do they share needs?) - Word-of-mouth potential (do they talk to each other?) - Defensibility (can you build a moat here?) ### Step 5: Kano Landscape Assessment For each candidate population, assess the current solution landscape: 1. What do existing solutions provide as **Must-Be** (table stakes)? 2. What are the **Performance** dimensions competitors fight on? 3. **Where is the Attractive Quality gap?** (What would delight this population that nobody currently offers?) 4. **Paths A & B:** Populations where the Attractive Quality gap aligns with the user's means inventory score highest. 5. **Path C:** Assess the Attractive Quality gap on its own terms. Do NOT filter by means alignment here. Means alignment is scored separately in Step 6. ### Step 6: Market Attractiveness Scoring **Path C — Deferred Means Load:** NOW load the Means Inventory (same process as Step 1: load the Means Inventory page, construct via three questions, present for user confirmation). Use the confirmed means ONLY to score Means Alignment below. All other dimensions were scored means-free. Score each candidate population on a 1-5 scale across six dimensions:
**Dimension****What You're Scoring****Framework Source****Weight**
**Problem Severity**How acute is the unmet need? Do workarounds exist? (Workarounds = strong signal)Lead User, Customer Discovery25%
**Means Alignment**How well do the user's **transferable capabilities** (primary) and domain knowledge/network (secondary) match this population's needs? Score against the Transferable Capabilities table first. A population outside the user's professional industry can score 3-4 if multiple transferable capabilities genuinely match its core unmet needs. Use the "How to Score Means Alignment" rubric in the Means Inventory page.Effectuation20%
**Accessibility**Can you reach, study, and serve this population with current resources?Effectuation, Beachhead15%
**Market Size**Is this population large enough to sustain a viable offering?Market Attractiveness15%
**Competition Gap**Are existing solutions failing them? Is there an Attractive Quality gap?Nonconsumption, Kano15%
**Trend Momentum**Is this population growing? Is the underlying trend accelerating?Lead User, Adoption Lifecycle10%
**Composite Score** = Weighted average (gives 1.0-5.0 scale) **Interpretation:** - **4.0-5.0:** High-conviction target. Pursue immediately. - **3.0-3.9:** Promising. Worth deeper investigation. - **2.0-2.9:** Weak signal. Park or pivot. - **1.0-1.9:** No compelling evidence. Archive. ### Step 7: Shortlist & Proto-Persona Output Rank the top 3-5 populations by composite score. For each: 1. **Population Name** (descriptive label) 2. **Who They Are** (role, context, environment, scale) 3. **Why They're a Target** (which framework(s) identified them, with reasoning) 4. **The Unmet Need Signal** (what evidence points to unaddressed demand) 5. **Attractiveness Score** (with dimension breakdown) 6. **Proto-Persona Sketch** (enough detail to feed into 💡 Feature Ideator): - Who (role, context) - What (goals, tasks) - Why (motivations, frustrations) - Where/When (context of use) 7. **Recommended Next Step** ("Feed this proto-persona into Feature Ideation" or "Conduct discovery interviews first") --- ## Methodological Constraints - Every population recommendation must trace to at least one validated framework - Trend identification must be grounded in web search evidence, not speculation - Analogous market search (Lead User) is **mandatory**, not optional. Always look beyond the obvious market. - Nonconsumption scan must explicitly consider all three tiers of noncustomers - Means alignment scoring assesses how well the user's **transferable capabilities** (not just domain expertise) match the population's needs. Populations outside the user's professional domain can score 3-4 on Means Alignment if multiple transferable capabilities genuinely apply. Low domain knowledge and weak network access reduce the score but do not automatically disqualify a population. - Kano landscape assessment prevents recommending populations where the solution space is already saturated - Proto-personas are hypothetical. Flag them as requiring validation through Customer Discovery interviews. - Composite scoring is heuristic. Flag this limitation. --- ## Integration Points - **Upstream of 💡 Feature Ideator:** Selected populations become Feature Ideator input personas - **Upstream of Validate Business Idea:** Selected populations inform the "who it's for" dimension - **Composes with 📊 Analyst:** For deeper market research on specific populations - **Composes with 🎓 Academic:** For scholarly rigor in trend and framework application --- ## Source Library Primary sources grounding each framework step.
